Description  
Public demand can create early markets for circular products and services, reduce 
reliance on newly extracted materials, extend product life, strengthen secondary 
resource markets and advance Sustainable Development Goal 12. Internationally and 
in India, information instruments, combined with market-promotion instruments, have 
helped achieve environmental goals.  
  
Circular Public Procurement (CPP) enables governments to use their purchasing 
power to create demand for circular products and solutions. In India, where public 
procurement represents approximately 20–22% of GDP, it can serve as a significant 
demand-side instrument for encouraging sustainable production practices and the 
uptake of circular products.

India’s Ecomark programme has been restructured through the Ecomark Rules, 2024, 
while the draft amendments proposed in 2026 introduce stronger circularity 
considerations, including recycled content, Extended Producer Responsibility, 
lifecycle assessment and traceability. Information instruments, including ecolabels, 
need to be complemented by market-promotion instruments such as public 
procurement to encourage manufacturer uptake. Existing provisions under the 
General Financial Rules, 2017, including those relating to lifecycle costing, recognised 
standards and environmental characteristics, provide entry points for integrating these 
considerations into public procurement.  
  
Objectives  
The session will bring together stakeholders from government, industry and research 
to share perspectives and chart pathways for integrating environmental and 
sustainability criteria into public procurement.  
  
Through a framing presentation, panel discussion and interactive exercise, 
participants will identify actions for moving CPP from policy ambition to 
implementation.  
  
The session asks  
• 
What policy, institutional and market conditions are required to link information 
instruments with public procurement (a market promotion instrument)?  
• 
Which sectors and product categories are most ready for Circular Public 
Procurement?  
• 
How can standards, ecolabel and visual identifiers be integrated into 
procurement systems?  
  
Participants can look forward to practical examples and perspectives through an 
interactive discussion on translating circular-economy objectives into public 
purchasing decisions.
